# Pacific Run — independent art-direction review after the water pass

Review date: 2026-10-05. This is the one authorized review of the supplied current candidate. Scores follow `artifacts/water/CRITIC_BRIEF.md`; previous grades and the 9.3 target were not used as scoring anchors.

**Overall visual quality: 7.6/10. Water quality: 8.0/10.**

The candidate has a coherent, appealing coastal-drive identity and several strong finished elements, but the environment still reads as a well-developed prototype with conspicuous craft gaps. Water is stronger than the overall scene: its color, surface response and rock reflections support the relaxed atmosphere, while its shore integration and larger-scale form remain visibly simplified. These are more than the minor gaps associated with the rubric's 9 anchor.

## Score rationale

**Composition and route readability are strong.** Center lines, white road edges, guardrails and utility lines carry the eye into the bend in `coast.png`, `station.png` and `hood.png`. The car is an immediate focal point, while the lighthouse gives the coastal stretch an identifiable destination. The desktop garage uses the sea and diagonal road well behind its title and vehicle presentation. The warm cream/gold interface, vehicle paint and pale afternoon light belong to the same visual world.

**Atmosphere works, but the lighting loses detail.** The forest view offers a pleasing contrast between dark trees and warm light on the sea-facing side. The open horizon and calm water fit a relaxing drive. However, coast, forest and sunset share a similar broad pale sky and road/slope arrangement; sunset is principally a warmer version of that composition. In `high-forest-drive.png` and `high-shoreline.png`, the bright horizon washes trees and distant scene detail toward cream. Clouds show regular horizontal comb-like bands in the close-water and hood views. This is noticeable procedural structure in a large part of the frame, rather than a finished atmospheric sky.

**Environment craft is uneven.** Asphalt, lane reflectors, roadside stones, the lighthouse and the station's small furnishings add useful detail. The station dog and visitor give the stop some life, and the lodge has a recognizable roadside-motel silhouette. Against that, long slopes remain broad textured surfaces with repeated isolated palm/plant placements. The forest has more density but still exposes repeated tree silhouettes and a sparse floor. The station and lodge occupy crisp rectangular pads with little surrounding grade, landscaping or access transition, making them feel placed on the terrain. Large beach rocks are dark, smooth blobs beside the more faceted water rocks. These differences are visible in the same shoreline composition and weaken a common level of craft.

**Object grounding needs more consistency.** The metallic car is attractive from behind and its rims, vents and trim read clearly, but the hero shots give little visible contact shadow beneath the tires. The phone drive includes a conspicuous dark block-shaped shadow ahead of the car. This assessment concerns the visible shadow appearance only; the frames do not establish its cause. Bright reflective paint, simplified architecture, thin palm fronds and broadly shaded beach rocks are not yet equally convincing under the shared light.

**Water is a successful focal material.** `high-water-close.png` and `motion-water.png` show directional surface ripples that deform the rock reflections without destroying their readability. `high-shoreline.png` and `high-headland-shore.png` give the coast a useful turquoise-to-silver depth and distance change. The paired Balanced views retain the essential look; the main visible difference in these examples is less warm glow. The surface supports a calm-coast mood rather than looking like a solid flat blue fill.

The remaining water gaps are clearly visible from higher views: dense small ripples dominate nearly the whole surface, larger swell shapes are understated, and the sea meets the beach at a rather crisp edge with a lace-like foam band. Sand does not show a convincing wet transition. The water is attractive in isolation but its contact with the land is less finished. Exported frames 9 and 14 show changed surface/foam patterns, which supports that the material is animated; individual frames cannot establish natural motion or stable reflection cadence.

**Small-screen visual hierarchy is workable but crowded.** The phone garage retains a strong title, chapter button and car, although vehicle information is laid over the car. In `phone-drive.png`, the large car, radio/minimap overlays and speed display compete in the lower half. Pale upper-left text loses contrast against the bright sky. This is a visual-readability observation, not an accessibility or touch-usability verdict.

## Strongest elements

- Clear road-led compositions and an unmistakable coast-driving identity, with the lighthouse and roadside stops supplying memorable focal points.
- A restrained warm palette that connects the metallic vehicle, afternoon light and garage/interface presentation.
- Turquoise shallows, directional ripples and reflected rock silhouettes: the water now contributes substantial atmosphere and depth to the sampled coast.
- Distinct palm and conifer sections plus a cyclist, visitor and dog make the route feel inhabited, even though broader environment variety is still limited.

These are strengths of the current candidate. A precise improvement delta is not claimed because this review did not inspect the prior candidate side by side.

## Three highest-value remaining visual fixes

1. **Author stronger terrain and location variation.** Break the long smooth slopes and regularly spaced vegetation into deliberate rock/plant groupings, exposed cuts, sheltered pockets and more distinct shoreline shapes. Give the headlands, forest and open coast different large silhouettes and spatial rhythms. Cut roadside stops into the terrain with convincing entrances, graded edges and localized dressing; `station-detail.png` and `lodge-detail.png` show why this would yield more than simply adding extra props.
2. **Unify lighting, sky and object grounding.** Preserve the appealing warm light while recovering detail around the horizon and backlit foliage. Replace the obvious horizontal cloud bands with more varied atmospheric shapes. Make tire, building and rock contact shadows consistent with their forms, and resolve the detached block-like shadow visible in `phone-drive.png`. The hero shots and landmark details would benefit immediately from a common level of grounding and material response.
3. **Finish the water-to-land transition.** Add a believable wet-sand zone, irregular shore foam and localized rock contact behavior, supported by larger gentle surface forms so the water is not dominated by one scale of fine ripples. Preserve the current turquoise color and readable reflections. The largest return is along the beach edge in `high-shoreline.png` and `motion-shore.png`, where the clean boundary currently exposes the simplified scene construction.

## Scope and gate verdict

**The reviewed graphics do not reach the 9.3 visual gate.** Overall art direction is 7.6 and water is 8.0. The result is attractive and coherent enough to communicate its intended mood, but environment variation, atmosphere, grounding and shore craft require more than minor finish work to justify an excellent score.

This verdict covers the supplied sampled graphics only. It does not regrade driving, completeness, audio, accessibility, input behavior or supported-device performance, and it does not establish overall player-test readiness. Phone images demonstrate a narrow viewport render, not live device behavior. Film and motion evidence was assessed as still frames; continuous animation, pacing, reflection updates and sustained visual stability remain outside this inspection. No further revision or second grading round follows this panel under the current authorization.
